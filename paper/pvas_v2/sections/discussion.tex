\section{Discussion and Limitations}\label{sec:disc}

\paragraph{One trajectory measures within-mode spread; the spectrum
prices the rest.}
$\PV$ estimates only the within-mode term of Proposition~1
(Eq.~\ref{eq:ltv}); it is blind to multi-modal ambiguity by
construction. The consequences are measured, not hypothesized:
chance-level ranking on from-scratch inpainting
(Table~\ref{tab:inpaint}) against a real but bounded signal in guided
SR (Fig.~\ref{fig:calibration})\RLOCAL. The same decomposition is
constructive. Each additional synchronized trajectory contributes an
unbiased sample of the cross-mode term; \pvas-duo is the two-sample
point and already restores active selection where one trajectory fails
($+0.94$\,dB over static in the $n{=}16$ inpainting pilot,
Sec.~\ref{sec:exp_inpaint})\RLOCAL, while $N$-particle ensembles
estimate the full sum and stay strongest under severe multi-modality at
$N\times$ cost. We claim recovery of full posterior uncertainty
nowhere; the claim is the cost--robustness ladder with its prices
measured (Table~\ref{tab:nfe_cost}).

\paragraph{Where adaptivity helps.}
The margins track the task's remaining headroom, not the policy. At
$R{=}4$ the MRI margin over static compresses when the budget doubles
($+0.37$ to $+0.11$\,dB, n.s.; gate-on grid, $n{=}30$,
Sec.~\ref{sec:exp_mri})\RLOCAL{} --- consistent with Bakker et
al.~\cite{bakker2022multicoil} --- but grows at $R{=}8$ ($+0.55$ to
$+1.16$\,dB)\RLOCAL{} and on CIFAR SR ($+0.85$ to $+3.86$\,dB,
Fig.~\ref{fig:budget})\RLOCAL, where the oracle headroom widens. It
also depends on sampler configuration, most visibly $\sigma_{\max}$
(supplement).

\paragraph{Retrospective protocol; what remains open.}
All MRI experiments retrospectively subsample fully acquired k-space,
standard practice \cite{bakker2020greedy,pineda2020rl}. Prospective use
adds sequence latency, ordering, and motion constraints, and
retrospective \psnrm{}/\ssimm{} gains establish no clinical benefit.
Every ensemble comparison in this paper is our own reimplementation of
the ADS/AdaSense-style disagreement signal, run head-to-head in the
shared pipeline at matched budget and prior
(Sec.~\ref{sec:method_active}); still open are head-to-head runs of the
authors' released code
\cite{elata2024adasense,nolan2025ads,iollo2025bedmri}, a prospective
MRI evaluation, and a CT instantiation on LoDoPaB-CT.

Finally, small $q$ works only because the noisy Hutchinson estimate is
aggregated per atom (Sec.~\ref{sec:method_pv}), a variance-for-bias
trade; zero-extra-cost covariance trackers \cite{rissanen2025freehunch}
driving the same selection rule are important future work.

\section{Conclusion}\label{sec:conclusion}

\pvas{} makes a passive training-free diffusion solver active for a
handful of extra denoiser calls: posterior variance read from the
trajectory's own denoiser ranks candidate measurements
mid-reconstruction, beating matched-budget static and random policies
--- and the passive solver it builds on --- at $1.24$--$1.4\times$
passive \nfe. Where one trajectory is not enough, Proposition~1 says
exactly what is missing; \pvas-duo restores it at ${\approx}2\times$,
and a particle ensemble remains the top of the ladder at $N\times$ ---
a spectrum evaluated under matched budget and matched \nfe, with the
ensemble baseline reimplemented and run head-to-head rather than
cited. We hope \nfe-matched evaluation becomes the norm for active
sensing.
